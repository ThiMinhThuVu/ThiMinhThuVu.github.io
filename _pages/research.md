# Research

My research connects evidence retrieval and biomedical question answering with multimodal reasoning for surgical video. The long-term goal is to develop surgical AI that is not only accurate, but also evidence-grounded, anatomically coherent, and clinically trustworthy.

## Surgical Scene Understanding

I study structured representations of surgical scenes that connect **anatomical structures, surgical instruments, actions, interaction targets, temporal relationships, and surgical workflow**. These representations can help a model reason about what is happening, how scene elements interact, and how an operation evolves over time.

Current directions include:

- Surgical Scene Graph Generation
- Temporal Scene Graph Modeling
- Instrument–Action–Target Reasoning
- Surgical Workflow Understanding
- Anatomically Consistent Scene Understanding

## Surgical Video Question Answering

Surgical questions often require more than recognizing a single frame. I investigate VideoQA systems that perform **temporal and causal reasoning, action understanding, intention or goal reasoning, and visual evidence localization**.

My central direction is **Evidence-grounded Surgical VideoQA**: a system should retrieve the relevant temporal evidence supporting its answer, rather than produce an answer without verifiable visual grounding. This brings together my earlier work in evidence retrieval and biomedical QA with surgical video understanding.

## Trustworthy Surgical AI

I am interested in evaluation and analysis that move beyond pixel-level metrics or aggregate accuracy. One current direction examines safety-aware segmentation errors through **model uncertainty and confidence, anatomical relationships, and structured surgical knowledge**. The aim is to distinguish routine errors from errors that may be more clinically consequential.

This is ongoing research; methods and findings described here should not be interpreted as peer-reviewed results.
